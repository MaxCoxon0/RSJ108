Final Challenge, Spring 2025, JOUR 108

How to effectively cut something out in Photoshop and use transparency

Assembly time: 10-20 minutes
Awesome rating: 80% awesome

A reoccurring issue that media designers have ran into for as long as the internet has been around is that so many viable and usable images are deemed unusable based off their lack of freedom from the already existing contents of an image. In this tutorial, we will go into how to crop out certain objects, or make certain backgrounds disappear, using the tools in Photoshop!

  • Things You'll Need

  • Any copy of Adobe Photoshop
  • Yourself

  1. Choose an image to edit in Photoshop and open the software.

    First, you'll want to find an image which contents' will be helpful for your design. Below, I've included an image I would typically choose to edit.

  2. Use the lasso tool and it's variations.

    In Photoshop, your true best friend for cutting out objects is the Lasso Tool and its' family, such as the Magnetic Lasso Tool and Polygonal Lasso Tool. To find these last two, simply hold click on the Lasso tool icon and drag to your desired variable.

    The red circle highlights where you will find the lasso tool on the toolbar.

  3. Begin cutting with the Lasso tool of your choice.

    Now drag your mouse over the edges of the object you want to crop out and separate from the rest of the image. With boxier objects that have clear edges and corner, the Polygonal Lasso tool will come in handy, and for objects that are easily differentiated from their background, the Magnetic Lasso tool will be ideal. If neither of these conditions occur, you may have to trace it manually. But there are a couple more methods to cropping out images...

  4. When to utilize "quick selection" and the "magic wand" tool.

    Oftentimes, the background could just potentially be one color, or a series of objects that are clearly different from the background, and it's easier to just get rid of the background as opposed to removing your primary object. Here are some examples:

  5. Transform your new design.

    After taking or removing what you need, you can play around with your new cut-outs further; using the "opacity" feature, you can make things more or less transparent. Further on you can warp your image or add various blending filters.
